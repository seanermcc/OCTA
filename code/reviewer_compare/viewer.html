Measurement definitions, source files, and limitations

Distance is the axial separation at the same native A-line: (Shichu − lead) × 1.12 µm/pixel. Positive bias means Shichu placed the boundary deeper. This measures agreement, not accuracy; neither reviewer is presumed correct. B-scan indices are zero-based, matching the original reviewer.

Confirmed scope uses only positions approved by both current, validated whole-B-scan confirmations, after visibility, reliability, anatomy, image exclusion, geometry and vessel/shadow guards. Drawn scope uses only reliable, non-displaced human strokes from both journals; it can include saved drafts. All-saved scope includes finite on-image curves, including unreviewed and explicitly denied positions, and is diagnostic only. Missing comparisons stay blank.

Both reviewers started from the same frozen model. Agreement on unchanged predictions is not independent human delineation. Compare the drawn-only scope as well; its coverage is much smaller. The pooled summary weights each paired boundary/A-line equally; nearby A-lines are correlated, so this is descriptive and supplies no confidence interval.

“Eligibility differs” counts A-lines where only one reviewer qualifies under the selected scope. It is shown separately from positional difference. Retinal boundary curves are compared here; CNV regions and CNV edges are displayed separately from these scores. Regions are lateral spans, not tissue masks. CNV edge coordinates and uncertainty are read from each reviewer’s saved journal; drafts are labeled. Hyper_Ref shows the exact saved brush footprints, with separate reviewer toggles. The lesion confirmation status covers CNV and Hyper_Ref together; draft paint is explicitly identified. Painted pixels are not a count of individual dots.

Images are already canonical, vitreous toward depth zero. Their saved crop offset is applied to full-depth journal positions; no registration, interpolation or independent flipping is used. Both panes share the same contrast and viewport. Grayscale uses each slice's 1st–99.5th percentiles; this affects display only.
